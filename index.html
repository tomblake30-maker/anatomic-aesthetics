Why I started Anatomic

Booking takes nerve. Walking in shouldn't.

My partner's first anti-wrinkle treatment should have been simple. She had a consultation, was sent home, and waited three days for someone else to prescribe it. Then she went back, paid a second fee, and had it done in a salon between the nails and the tans. Nobody explained why.

On her next visit, a man was sitting in the waiting room. He looked like he wanted to leave. In a room built for someone else, with nobody to tell him what would happen or whether it was safe, every minute must have made him doubt a decision he'd taken a long time to reach.

That's why plenty of men who want this never book. Walking past a high-street window into an open room, to ask a stranger about your face, feels exposed. Asking who is actually qualified feels awkward. So they leave it.

[I couldn't find anywhere in Norwich built for men,] so I built one: a private room in a medical building, and one clinician who assesses, prescribes and treats you, with everything explained before anything happens.

Who is allowed to treat you

A prescription needs a prescriber.
Some anti-wrinkle treatments use prescription-only medicines. A qualified prescriber should assess you in person first.
Fillers need no qualification at all.
In England, anyone can legally inject them today. A licensing scheme is planned but not yet in force.

Asked often

Will people be able to tell?

They should notice you look rested, not treated. If something would look obvious on you, Sam will say so.

Does it hurt?

A brief sting. Many fillers contain a local anaesthetic.

How long do results last?

Anti-wrinkle treatment: three to four months. Fillers: six to eighteen months, depending on area and product.

Can it be reversed?

Most dermal fillers are hyaluronic acid, which can be dissolved with an enzyme injection, sometimes over more than one session. Anti-wrinkle treatment cannot be dissolved; it wears off gradually over around three to four months. [Sam uses dissolvable hyaluronic acid fillers only.]

Is this an NHS service?

No. Anatomic is a private clinic and all treatment is paid for privately. Sam brings his NHS clinical experience to it, but the clinic is not part of the NHS.

Do I have to book treatment at the consultation?

No. You leave with a written plan, not a commitment.

Is it confidential?

Yes. Your records are held securely under UK GDPR and shared with no one, including your GP, without your consent.

Where are you?

A private consulting room in a Norwich medical building. The address comes with your booking.
